The problem

Disruption decisions are difficult

A supplier, route, port, or fuel-cost change can affect downstream deliveries and inventory. Teams need a clearer way to explore scenarios and compare possible responses.

The product

Model the network before acting

Users represent facilities and transport links, run what-if scenarios, examine modeled effects, and organize follow-up mitigation actions in one workspace.

How to explain it in 3 steps

1

Map

Set up suppliers, plants, ports, warehouses, customers, inventory, and routes.

2

Simulate

Describe a disruption—such as a facility closure or fuel shock—and compare modeled scenarios.

3

Respond

Review impacts, discuss options with the assistant, and track selected mitigation actions.

Project stage: Local-first MVP with deployment configuration prepared. A public hosted application has not yet been deployed.

01 / PRODUCTCapabilities & workflows
A connected operational workflow

From network data to an actionable review

The product combines supply-network modeling, scenario analysis, supplier monitoring, and team follow-through. It is designed to make assumptions and missing data visible—not to silently fill gaps.

Digital-twin workspace

Represent the supply network

  • Create company workspaces and guide setup from supplier to customer.
  • Represent suppliers, plants, ports, warehouses, distribution centers, customers, and routes.
  • Review the network visually and inspect route and facility details.
  • Keep synthetic demo networks clearly distinguished from company data.
What-if simulation

Explore disruption scenarios

  • Model facility or route closures and fuel-cost changes.
  • Recalculate a feasible lower-cost route when the network allows one.
  • Compare 2–4 scenarios and review modeled cost, delay, inventory, and stockout exposure.
  • Use risk labels as scenario indicators, not guarantees of real-world outcomes.
Supplier operations

Monitor reported performance

  • Store dated scorecards for on-time delivery, lead time, defects, and capacity utilization.
  • Preview and validate CSV/Excel reports before importing valid rows.
  • Compare trends, flag configured threshold breaches, and export portfolio data.
  • Show missing or outdated reports explicitly; do not grade unreported suppliers.
Control Tower & collaboration

Turn review into follow-through

  • Track mitigation actions with owners, due dates, status, modeled impact, and actual outcome notes.
  • Share workspaces using role-based owner, editor, and viewer access.
  • Review workspace activity and account notifications.
  • Compare recorded outcomes with modeled estimates after actions are completed.

Data quality is part of the workflow

Readiness checks
Check connectivity, route endpoints, inventory/demand inputs, cost/capacity coverage, and source references.

Transparent provenance
Mark examples as synthetic and user-submitted information as reported—not independently verified.

Review before use
Use estimates to support investigation; confirm assumptions with verified operational data.

02 / AI AGENTRole, workflow & boundaries
Local, contextual, advisory

What the Supply Chain Agent does

The agent is a conversational assistant embedded in the app. It helps people understand the product and their selected workspace, interpret modeled results, and prepare network changes for human review.

A

Answer questions

Responds to questions about app workflows and supply-chain concepts. When relevant, it receives the selected company’s active network context.

B

Help describe scenarios

A local model parses natural-language disruptions into structured scenario inputs. A deterministic fallback can extract supported details when the model is unavailable.

C

Explain simulation output

Summarizes route and inventory impact and suggests mitigation ideas. The language model explains results; it is instructed not to recalculate or change simulation values.

D

Draft network records

Can propose a supplier, facility, or route draft from a conversation. The user reviews the draft and explicitly saves it through the normal app flow.

Voice and privacy approach

Voice input is transcribed with a locally installed Whisper model on the backend. The recording is sent to the local backend for transcription; the design does not use browser speech recognition or send audio to a hosted transcription provider. Company reference documents are intended for local storage and are not parsed by or sent to an external service.

What the agent does not do

  • It does not autonomously modify or save the company network, supplier scorecards, or mitigation actions.
  • It does not independently verify uploaded or user-entered operational facts.
  • It does not guarantee predictions. Scenario outputs are estimates based on the current network data and modeled assumptions.
  • It is not currently part of the prepared hosted pilot: the assistant expects a privately reachable Ollama service, while the local computer’s Ollama endpoint cannot be reached from Render.
Plain-language positioning: AI helps users ask questions, structure scenarios, and understand results. The simulation engine calculates the modeled numbers; a person decides what to do.
